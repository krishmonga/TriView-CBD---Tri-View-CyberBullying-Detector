\begin{abstract}
Detecting cyberbullying in short social-media posts is difficult because insults, context, and word order all carry signal, and no single encoder captures each equally well. Most existing classifiers pick one route---TF-IDF or bag-of-words features, CNN n-grams, LSTM sequence models, or Transformer context---and ignore the rest. TriFuse learns three text views in parallel: a CNN for local patterns, a Transformer for token context, and a BiLSTM for sequence order, all fed from shared embeddings. Cross-view attention updates each branch, and a fusion layer with learnable temperature sets the branch weights at classification time instead of using fixed concatenation. Tests on the Shahane corpus (Dataset~I, 229,228 posts) and the Davidson hate-speech corpus (Dataset~II, 24,783 tweets), with matched preprocessing, splits, and training settings, give 94.32\% accuracy and 94.12\% F1 on Dataset~I and 95.87\% accuracy and 95.91\% F1 on Dataset~II. TriFuse outperforms Random Forest, LightGBM, CNN, BiLSTM, and Tuned LSTM under both single-split and stratified 5-fold evaluation. Ablations on both datasets show that the full three-branch fusion beats single-view, two-branch, and late-fusion variants.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Cyberbullying detection; Multi-view representation learning; Adaptive fusion; Deep learning; Text classification; Cross-dataset evaluation
